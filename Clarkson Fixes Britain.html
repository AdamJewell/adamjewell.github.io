TV SERIES PITCH: CLARKSON FIXES BRITAIN Genre: Uplifting Engineering, Nature & Community Makeover Docuseries Format: 8 x 60-minute episodes (Seasonal Regional Anthology) Tone: Clarkson’s Farm meets Grand Designs — warm, funny, hands-on, and fiercely practical. THE LOGLINE After six years of battling floods, droughts, and red tape on his own farm, Jeremy Clarkson assembles a team of Britain’s sharpest ecologists, engineers, and problem-solvers to tackle "Broken Britain" at scale—adopting one struggling river valley per season to fix its flooding, polluted rivers, lost nature, and cut-off transport all at once. THE PREMISE & "WHY NOW?" Clarkson’s Farm diagnosed the problem: Britain’s climate is becoming more extreme, working the land is nearly impossible, and disjointed rules are strangling the countryside. Clarkson Fixes Britain is the prescription. Instead of preaching about climate change or getting bogged down in Westminster blame games, this show uses entertainment budgets to fund real-world, common-sense fixes on the ground. It strips away culture-war politics to prove a simple truth: fixing Broken Britain and restoring British nature are the exact same job. THE FORMAT: THE "MISERY MATRIX" & WAR ROOM 1. The Data Audit: In a central "War Room," a systems-thinking meta-expert and specialist researchers overlay national datasets—flood risk, river pollution, lack of public green space, and lost rural transport links—onto a giant map of the UK to find towns suffering from all of them at once. 2. One Valley, One Blueprint: For Season 1, Jeremy picks one overlapping river valley (from upland hills down to a market town) and deals with one local council. Because everything in a catchment connects, viewers see how fixing the hills saves the town downstream. 3. Parallel Builds: Rather than isolating one issue per episode, four interlocking projects run simultaneously across the season—cutting between muddy engineering breakthroughs, wildlife milestones, and comic collisions with local bureaucracy. 4. The Nationwide Blueprint: Every cost, legal workaround, and engineering fix is published openly so other British towns and farmers can copy-paste the blueprint without waiting years for government action. THE ENSEMBLE CAST • Jeremy Clarkson (The Sceptical Ringmaster): Puts his own reputation and graft on the line, translating complex ecological and engineering solutions into plain English for everyday viewers. • The Superstar Coordinator (The New "Charlie"): A pragmatic, unflappable project director who bridges the gap between ambitious science, council paperwork, and tight budgets. • The Familiar Face: The bird and wildlife specialist from Clarkson’s Farm Season 5, tracking the emotional return of vanished British species as habitats heal. • The Specialist Crew: A rotating cast of passionate experts—hydrologists, beaver biologists, silvopasture foresters, and lightweight rail engineers—paired with sceptical local farmers and residents who slowly become the show’s biggest champions. • Diddly Squat Cameos: Occasional drop-ins from Kaleb Cooper and Charlie Ireland as the voice of working-farmer reality. SEASON 1: THE FOUR PILLARS (8-EPISODE ARC) • Pillar 1: Upstream Rewilding & Flood Defence Re-wetting degraded upland peat bogs, re-wiggling straightened rivers, and introducing beavers to slow storm water naturally before it floods the town high street. • Pillar 2: Cleaning the River & Silvopasture Working alongside local livestock farmers to plant silvopasture shelterbelts (trees integrated into grazing pasture) and build natural reed beds that stop agricultural runoff and revive dying rivers. • Pillar 3: Access to Wild Nature Transforming neglected land on the edge of town into a thriving, public-access wild woodland and riverside trail—giving locals free green space for health, exercise, and seeing rewilding up close. • Pillar 4: Reconnecting Lost Transport Reclaiming an old Beeching-cut railway branch line or rural corridor to test Very Light Rail (VLR)—lightweight, autonomous battery-electric shuttle pods that reconnect a car-choked town to the mainline cheaply and cleanly. THE FINALE PAYOFF Like the big reveal on Grand Designs, Episode 8 brings the whole valley together: sceptical locals ride the new electric rail shuttle, families walk the newly opened riverside woodland, kingfishers return to clean water, and when winter storms hit, the town stays dry. Season 2 then moves to a new British landscape (such as a struggling coastal fishing town) while checking back in on Season 1’s progress.
